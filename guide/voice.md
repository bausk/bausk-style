# Voice

How text reads in Oleksandr's work, in English and Ukrainian.

## Register

An engineer and lecturer explaining something to a capable colleague or student: direct, precise,
unhurried, a little dry. First person singular where it is his work ("I tested three detectors").
Claims come with a number, a source or an example.

## Rules of thumb

- Concrete nouns and active verbs. "RANSAC rejects 38 % of matches", not "RANSAC helps improve robustness".
- One idea per sentence; vary sentence length.
- Numbers with units and absolute dates: 14 min, 1920×1080, 11 Oct 2026 / 11 жовтня 2026.
- Name things as the reader knows them: "watchlist", not "symbol collection entity".
- UI labels are short verbs or nouns in sentence case: "Add symbol", «Додати тікер». A control keeps its name through the flow (button "Publish" → message "Published").
- Errors say what happened and what to do: "IBKR did not answer for 2 min. Retrying; data on screen is from 14:02."
- Empty states invite the next action: "No symbols yet. Add one to start."
- No hype, no exclamation marks, no apologies from the interface.

## Ukrainian specifics

- Quotes «…», with „…“ inside.
- Spaced en/em dash « — » between parts of a sentence; unspaced en dash in ranges: 2024–2026.
- Decimal comma in prose (2,5 с), narrow no-break space in thousands (1 284).
- Abbreviations: «рис.», «табл.», «с.»; a non-breaking space after «рис.» and before units.
- Avoid calques from English marketing ("рішення" for everything, «в рамках», «на даний момент»).
- Terms: keep the established Ukrainian term and give the English in brackets on first use: гомографія (homography).
